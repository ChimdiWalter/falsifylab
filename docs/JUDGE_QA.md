# Answers to skeptical judging questions

Each answer points to evidence that can be checked in the repository.

**1. Isn't this just POPPER?**
No. POPPER (Huang et al., ICML 2025) runs agentic sequential falsification with strict
Type-I error control over free-text hypotheses. FalsifyLab has no calibrated error control;
its thresholds are heuristics. What it adds is narrower and physical. It locates *where*
in a controllable parameter (strain, amplitude, horizon) a fitted law fails, revises within
a declared family, enforces a model freeze in code, and exports counted, hash-linked
measurements. See `docs/CLAIM_NOVELTY_TABLE.md`.

**2. Isn't this just curve fitting?**
The fits are deliberately simple. The workflow around them is the point:
- a counted experiment budget;
- an explicit counterexample;
- a validity boundary with abstention;
- a revision frozen before any audit data exists;
- negative controls that must fail, such as the damped oscillator, where no conservation
  law may be certified.

**3. Where is the new physical discovery?**
There is none, and none is claimed. Hooke's law, bilinear hardening and the Duffing
invariant are textbook physics. The benchmarks validate the *method* on systems with
known answers, which is the only way to score whether it found the right boundary.

**4. Isn't the bisection comparison rigged?**
Partly, and we say so. Bisection's 5.1 × 10⁻⁵ bracket is its design resolution
(0.013/2⁸), which holds whenever its monotonic assumption holds. The "28× finer than a
grid" figure is arithmetic. The measured content is coverage and abstention:
- 30/30 at nominal noise;
- 17/29 at 8 MPa;
- post-hoc, 38 of 426 brackets at 0.8–16 MPa missed the onset, all in settings where the
  engine abstained on every run. Abstention is conservative, not discriminating: at 2 MPa
  bisection also abstained on 26/30 correct brackets.

A hidden softening dip makes bisection fail silently, and the mean-error audit then passes the
revision too (`docs/INDEPENDENT_AUDIT.md` §3.5 and §8).

**5. Does the search improve the revised model?**
For the material, no. All three policies reach the same 0.40 MPa audit error, which is
the measurement floor (0.37 MPa). For the oscillator under noise, yes, partly:
- the discovery-only quartic fails 25/30 and 30/30 audits;
- a random extra experiment fails 8/30 and 14/30;
- the search-selected experiment is never refuted, but it is confirmed in only 4/60 runs and
  inconclusive in the rest.

The comparison is not budget-matched: the search spent 15 catalog runs plus 1, and one
largest-amplitude run with no search ties it (post-hoc, `audit/posthoc_oscillator_excitation.json`).

**6. Are 30 seeds 30 independent experiments?**
No.
- For the material, they are 30 noise realizations of one simulated material.
- For the oscillator, primary runs are noise-free. Discovery, search and revision are
  identical across seeds, which only jitter the audit amplitudes.

The docs and the frontend state both.

**7. Why synthetic data?**
Ground truth is needed to score whether a boundary or revision is right. The engine only
sees an observation callable, so a real instrument could replace the simulator. That is
future work, not a demonstrated result.

**8. What is an "evidence certificate", and is it a proof?**
It is a schema-validated JSON record. It holds partitions, the model-freeze fingerprint,
every experiment and its cost, raw-data SHA-256 hashes and limitations.
`python -m falsifylab.verify outputs` recomputes the metrics from the raw arrays.

It shows internal consistency, not truth, and it is not a proof. A self-consistent
fabricated run would also verify. The stronger check is re-execution: an independent
rerun reproduced 184 of 187 benchmark files byte for byte, including all 174 raw arrays; the
other three differ only in provenance fields (`docs/logs/audit/benchmark-rerun-comparison.txt`).

**9. How do you know the browser demo is the real engine?**
The LIVE panels use a JavaScript port. `node site/tests/engine_parity.mjs` replays 42
recorded Python runs and 3 oscillator cases through it, and 1,500/1,500 decisions,
brackets, fits and audit errors match. Its random numbers differ from NumPy's, so the page
labels live runs as exploratory. The marimo notebook drives the actual Python engine.

**10. Were thresholds tuned on the test data?**
No evidence of it. The v3 protocol, seeds and thresholds were frozen at commit `63c686c`,
and the scientific modules are byte-identical from there to the audited checkpoint. Frozen
models re-derive exactly from the saved pre-freeze raw data, and the holdout is acquired
only after the freeze. Earlier v1/v2 holdouts are labelled development data. The freeze is
self-attested: the development history is not published, and the results were generated by
the same team shortly after the freeze. What a reader can check is that every evidence record
carries SHA-256 hashes of the scientific modules, which match the published source files
(check P in `audit/independent_checks.py`).

**11. What happens with heavy noise?**
Mostly it abstains. At 8 MPa and above, every bracket and every revised audit is marked
INCONCLUSIVE rather than reported as precise. But at 0.01 instrument noise the valid
harmonic control is never confirmed, and it is falsely refuted in 4/30 runs (2–4/30 across
horizons; `audit/posthoc_controls.json`). The abstention rule is also given the true
instrument noise SD, as from a calibration sheet.

**12. Why not compare with Gaussian-process level-set estimation, SINDy or PySR?**
There was no time to do it fairly before the deadline. It is listed as the
highest-value next step. The current baselines are only uniform and random sampling at an
equal budget.

**13. Was an LLM used?**
No language model runs in the pipeline or judges a physical claim. All verdicts come from
deterministic numerical code.

**14. What would a working scientist use this for?**
- To challenge a fitted or learned constitutive model before trusting it outside its
  calibration range.
- To get a reproducible record of which experiments were run, where the model failed, and
  when the data could not decide.

**15. Which sponsor tool was used?**
marimo (`notebooks/explore.py`), a reactive notebook over the real engine, tested live.
Valency and Adaption were not used and should not be selected.
